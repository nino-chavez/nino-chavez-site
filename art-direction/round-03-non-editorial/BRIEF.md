# Round 03 — non-editorial art-direction brief

## Why this round exists

Rounds 01 and 02 converged on one visual family: dark or paper-like portfolio
surfaces, oversized display typography, mono metadata, numbered sections,
ruled lists, and publishing or catalogue metaphors.

Those rounds are preserved as evidence, but all six directions are rejected.
Round 03 must change the visual premise rather than restyle the same premise.

## Fixed product argument

Use the product definition in `../../PRODUCT.md`.

The homepage is a public work surface for an agent-assisted practice across
software, sport, commerce, photography, media, and writing. The range is proof
that the practice works across domains. The interface should make real objects
reachable and their state honest.

Use the supplied volleyball, demo, and essay imagery where imagery materially
supports the direction. Do not use all three merely to fill space.

This is comparison copy, not final publication copy. Do not invent metrics,
clients, testimonials, awards, dates, availability, or project outcomes.

## Required divergence

Produce three directions with different spatial and behavioral premises, not
three typography or palette variations.

1. A **tool-like** direction: direct, responsive, and useful without mimicking
   a dashboard, terminal, operating system, or command palette.
2. A **spatial/media-led** direction: artifacts create the encounter without
   becoming a gallery wall, collage, exhibition, or editorial spread.
3. A **warm utilitarian** direction: approachable and physical without paper,
   print, notebook, workshop, filing, or catalogue metaphors.

Each direction must identify one signature behavior that could survive across
the homepage, work index, and project detail pages.

## Hard anti-brief

Reject the following even if executed beautifully:

- magazine, newspaper, book, issue, edition, dispatch, field-note, archive,
  exhibition, catalogue, contact-sheet, or index as the governing metaphor;
- serif or italic display type used as a shortcut to taste;
- mono type, tracked all-caps labels, section numbers, eyebrows, kickers, or
  specimen metadata;
- ruled ledgers, hairline-heavy lists, registration marks, crop marks, folios,
  paper grain, tape, stamps, or marginalia;
- a giant static statement paired with tiny supporting metadata;
- the dark creative-portfolio default: near-black ground, one neon accent,
  oversized grotesk, and generous empty space as the entire idea;
- bento grids, three equal cards, card walls, pills everywhere, or a uniform
  component repeated until the page is full;
- gradient meshes, blurred orbs, decorative grid backgrounds, crosshairs,
  fake charts, fake code, fake terminals, fake windows, or fake application
  chrome;
- portfolio headings such as "selected work," "featured projects," or "case
  studies";
- hiding honest state behind hover, clever copy, or unexplained color;
- motion that exists only to make a static layout feel designed.

## Positive steering

- Start from what the visitor can do and feel, not from a visual genre.
- Prefer a strong spatial rule, material behavior, or interaction model over a
  decorative signature.
- Use contemporary sans typography with normal casing and readable hierarchy.
  Type may be expressive, but it cannot carry the direction alone.
- Let color create orientation or energy rather than merely supplying an
  accent.
- Treat imagery as an active material with a defined role, not rectangular
  content dropped into cards.
- Make status legible in plain language and keep it subordinate to the work.
- Provide an immediate path into at least Blueprint, browse-tool, and Rally HQ.
- Let irregularity follow content meaning, not art-director whim.
- Design the first 1600×1000 frame as a real site surface, not a mood board or
  presentation slide.

## Impeccable-derived checks

Use the Impeccable sequence conceptually:

1. **Shape:** name the visitor mode, spatial premise, signature behavior, and
   visual system before styling the surface.
2. **Critique:** identify the first three places the direction could collapse
   into a familiar portfolio template.
3. **Distill:** remove any element that does not improve orientation, action,
   or the distinct premise.
4. **Polish:** remove generated-design tells and make the hierarchy decisive.

Do not run a second design vocabulary concurrently. Taste Skill contributes
only the rejection checks below; it does not direct the composition.

## Taste-derived preflight

Before returning an option, verify:

- one unmistakable primary encounter;
- no repeated section-intro pattern;
- no decorative microcopy;
- no arbitrary corner radii or container chrome;
- no decorative SVG;
- no invented interface or data;
- no equal-card default;
- no generic AI palette;
- no text smaller than 12px in the 1600×1000 specimen;
- no visual device included solely because it signals "designer."

## Comparison contract

- Medium: one self-contained HTML file per direction.
- Viewport: 1600×1000.
- Shared content: exactly the specimen content above.
- Shared assets: use copies of the supplied assets by relative path.
- No browser chrome or annotations in the rendered frame.
- Responsive down to 390px, although the picker compares the fixed viewport.
- Respect reduced motion.
- Semantic links and headings, visible focus, and sufficient contrast.
- Each option must return an implementation-ready brief covering:
  - premise and visitor mode;
  - signature behavior;
  - palette;
  - typography;
  - layout and responsive transformation;
  - imagery;
  - interaction and motion;
  - risks and anti-template checks.

## Selection criterion

Choose the direction that makes the body of work feel most alive, reachable,
and unmistakably authored without borrowing the authority of publishing,
galleries, developer tools, or creative-agency portfolios.
