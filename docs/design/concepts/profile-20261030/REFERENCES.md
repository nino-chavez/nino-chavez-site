## Can someone understand the destination without knowing our terminology?

Source: [Jared Rohrer's reaction to Graydon's website communication example](https://www.instagram.com/p/DeZw-SxhfC-/), 40 seconds. The clip uses an intentionally distracted novice as its clarity test. At approximately 8–10 seconds it compares a storage specification with a concrete description of how much music a device holds. At 27–35 seconds the speaker emphasizes immediate understanding of the offer and its benefit.

Transfer proposed: keep project names, but give each a concrete purpose nearby. Replace organizational abstractions such as “Source and publishing” with recognizable destinations or smaller plain groups. For this personal profile, adapt the principle to orientation and relevance; there is no evidence that the homepage should become a sales funnel. Do not import the video's market-share claim: it was not verified and is not needed here.

Target check: can a cold reader find photographs, understand what Rally HQ does, and find a contact route without reading the whole page? An observer's judgment is not an analytics measurement.

## How can grouping reduce the work of scanning?

Source: [Zen UX on cognitive load](https://www.instagram.com/p/DehgzGJt7mJ/), 14 seconds. The speaker links overwhelming interfaces to cognitive load and points to whitespace and chunking. The parent viewed frames at 4, 8, and 11 seconds; the last shows grouped speaker products with their information kept together.

Transfer proposed: group each name, description, and link; distinguish section spacing from item spacing; prioritize the first useful choices. The current site already has generous whitespace, so increasing all gaps would not follow from this reference. Avoid forcing the visitor to classify themselves or open a menu before seeing the content.

Target check: compare the same home and eleven-destination Links page across three structures at desktop and phone widths. Look for direct reading order, legible descriptions, recognizable actions, and a manageable page length. These videos offer design arguments, not controlled research proving our redesign will work.
