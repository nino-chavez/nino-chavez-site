# Require styling work; reopen the Work entrance

The shared domain still makes sense. The frontend needs a substantive styling refit, and Work's entrance deserves a scoped rethink comparison before its structure is locked. Moving existing blocks and fixing labels is insufficient. A wholesale replacement of the site's visual identity is not supported by these screens.

My original assessment was too protective of the current execution. It correctly identified responsive and navigation defects, but treated an authored identity as a stronger reason to preserve its presentation than the evidence allowed. The working plan now requires explicit styling work and leaves Work's organizing entrance open for comparison.

This is a judgment from rendered evidence, not a vote proving usability or conversion. The dissent on Work matters because it identifies a plausible structural remedy that my earlier plan excluded too early.

The archive repeatedly gives headings, labels, rules, and rows similar visual weight | Opening rhythm, featured-object proportions, archive density, metadata emphasis, rules, and filter/result treatment | Full archive access, publication identity, source ownership, correct counts and query URLs |
| Article arrival | Two menu layers, a back link, and a large illustration precede the title on a phone | Chrome height, image/title proportions, title scale, metadata spacing, and global/local navigation treatment | The article body, whose phone and desktop measure and rhythm are strong |
| Photography entrance | On the phone, the search slab covers the central subject of the image. The dark overlays suppress the photograph | Crop around the subject, directional rather than indiscriminate overlays, panel opacity/area, control styling, and heading/search balance | Search-first discovery, real images, complete event access |

These changes can keep the font families and owned color palette while changing their execution materially. “Preserve Anton / Inter / Space Mono” does not mean preserving every scale, weight, line break, or use of those faces. Keeping the palette does not mean keeping every overlay, surface relationship, or emphasis level.

Likewise, quieter rules are a candidate remedy where they compete with content, not a ban on borders. A text-only method does not need an invented screenshot to fill its card. Recompose the object rather than manufacture proof.

## Work earns a bounded structural comparison

The current opening teaches the classification system before showing a named artifact. The catalog then adds another layer of headings, filters, status, group introduction, and records. That may be useful orientation for someone seeking a domain. It is a less convincing default for someone asking what Nino has made.

The page does provide a visible “Browse all” shortcut. A visitor is not forced to read every category, and direct project referrals bypass this entrance entirely. Those facts weaken the reviewer's claim that everyone must decode the taxonomy. They do not settle whether the default composition earns the space it consumes.

Compare these three whole Work screens using the same authorized records, real statuses, and phone/desktop states:

1. **Compact domain map, then records.** The current organizing model with a substantive styling refit. This is the control and could still win.
2. **Domains containing named evidence.** Each domain exposes a concrete artifact and immediate access to its complete group. Breadth and proof arrive together.
3. **Records first, domains as browsing controls.** Named work is the initial object; the full domain map remains reachable and useful for refining the collection.

None may substitute a permanent prestige shortlist for the complete inventory. Keep query/filter URLs, clearing and empty states, and return context. Compare what a cold visitor understands, what they choose, and what they can inspect. Keep the rejected alternatives and deliberately fold useful details into the selected direction.

Options two and three may change the accepted presentation model. The current [functional owner](../../OPEN-PRACTICE-ART-DIRECTION.md) explicitly says “Work is an atlas before it is a registry.” This review does not amend that record. A selected alternative needs Nino's decision and a narrow amendment explaining the tradeoff. The same domain and separate applications can serve any of these structures.

## Corrections to the team's claims

Independent reports remain intact. These dispositions prevent their overstatements becoming new facts:

- **Work's first-record position:** `scrollY: 2026` is the start of the captured phone catalog slice, not the exact top of its first record. The first record appears further down that slice, after controls and group introduction. The deferral is visible; that coordinate must not be quoted as a measured first-record position.
- **Photography's panel size:** the panel obscures the central subject. It does not occupy a majority of the whole 844px viewport. Retain the composition criticism and reject the “most of the viewport” wording.
- **Album needs an IA rethink:** different local tools and dark/yellow styling do not establish a broken gallery model. Existing shared-navigation behavior and naming need repair. Preserve useful gallery-local navigation; treat common-shell styling as a refit unless observed behavior warrants more.
- **Coverage typography:** the rendered heading uses Anton on flat ground, while the general visual guide restricts Anton to photography. The later Coverage brief also deliberately chose Anton. This is a style-consistency question with approval history, not proof that the offer is unclear or its current price wrong. Compare it in the type pass; preserve current commercial terms and request behavior.
- **Below-entrance coverage:** the assessment reviewer finished without the additional slices. The visual and structure reviewers subsequently opened them, and the parent opened them directly. That first review is not evidence of those later frames.
- **Research limits:** none of this establishes improved conversion, physical-phone performance, assistive-technology behavior, or a winning replacement design. Weak analytics neither condemn the current styling nor certify it as adequate.

## What changes in the plan

The revised sequence is: repair the confirmed responsive/link defects; establish a common shell; perform a real styling refit; compare Work's structures before locking its entrance; measure Writing's browsing and delivery; then verify the combined release and search handoff.

Home's primary outcome remains an explicit human choice. Its photograph is identity material, not an immutable layout. Compare a small number of real Home compositions within the refit to resolve image/proof balance after that outcome is settled. A full-site concept exercise is unnecessary at this stage.

Writing's structural rethink remains conditional. Its long document is not by itself proof of poor retrieval or slow interaction. The added article-body captures strengthen the case for preserving reading typography while fixing the entry.

The working plan is **amended and not locked for implementation**. No website styling, content, routing, pricing, or production setting was changed in this review.

## What would change this decision

- The compact-map Work control clearly serves cold and referral tasks as well as the alternatives: keep its structure and complete the styling refit.
- The evidence-bearing or records-first Work screen gives a clearer connection between practice and artifacts without hiding breadth: select the bounded rethink and amend its owner.
- A real Home refit still cannot join personal identity with credible work: reopen its visual premise with three divergent concepts.
- Phone Writing tasks already work well and measured delivery is acceptable: stop at navigation and styling repairs.
- A common-shell change damages reading or photo retrieval: retain the local tools and correct the shared treatment.
