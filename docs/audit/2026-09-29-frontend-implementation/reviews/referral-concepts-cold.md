All three make the immediate job clear. Album pages name the event, date, photograph count, photographer, and gallery action. ## Blocking defects

The closest issue is **Keep Exploring album desktop**: the permanent left site navigation plus event-information column take roughly a third of the frame before the photographs begin. The gallery is still clear, but a referred visitor’s primary task is visually secondary. Smallest correction: reduce the event column width or let the grid begin closer to the left edge.

This is not a blocker; move the image below the first paragraph if first-read momentum matters more than the editorial opening.

## Tradeoffs

**Straight-to-it** is best for utility. Its album desktop has an unusually large empty dark field above the grid, especially around the right-aligned controls. That is an aesthetic imbalance, not a task failure. Tightening that vertical space would make the photo work arrive sooner. Its article is the plainest and most immediately readable, but has the least distinctive opening.

**By Nino** is the strongest visual presentation. The album’s hero photograph has generous scale and a good crop on both sizes; the article image provides a memorable entry point. The tradeoff is that both pages ask the visitor to take in more framing before acting or reading.

**Keep Exploring** gives the clearest sense that these pages belong to a wider personal site. On desktop, the visible “Work / Sessions / Learn / Writing / Photography / About” rail is useful orientation, but it competes with both reading and gallery browsing. On phone, the compact versions avoid that crowding well.

## Verdict

No candidate should be rejected for clarity or readability. Choose **Straight-to-it** if the priority is the fastest referred-visitor path. Choose **By Nino** if the redesign should make the work feel authored and visually considered; it is the strongest overall candidate. Keep Exploring is viable, but its desktop side rails are a functional cost to the immediate task and need the small width correction above.
